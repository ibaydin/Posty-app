There is a particular kind of quiet that only arrives when the calendar has nothing to say.

No errands to race through. No list folded in a pocket. Just a Sunday morning, a window left open, and the small permission to see where the day goes.

## Start with a walk

I left the house without headphones and took the long way toward the river. The neighborhood was doing its slow weekend thing: a dog negotiating with a leaf, coffee cups appearing on stoops, the smell of warm bread drifting out from the corner bakery.

> The best discoveries rarely announce themselves. They tend to be waiting just around the block.

On the way back, I bought a loaf I didn't need and found a tiny bookshop I'd walked past a dozen times. I stayed long enough to lose track of the hour.

## Make room for the unexpected

We spend so much time trying to make every day useful. But an unplanned afternoon can be useful in its own quiet way. It gives the mind space to wander, and wandering is often where the good ideas begin.

So here's a small suggestion: leave one square of the calendar empty this week. Let the day surprise you.

*Maybe bring home a loaf of bread.*
